# Task 2 — Conversion KPIs
### Calculate:
- **Website conversion rate.**
- **Cart abandonment rate.**

### Then answer:
- **Is a 3% conversion rate necessarily good or bad? Explain.**
- **Give two possible reasons for high cart abandonment.**



## 1. Website conversion rate :

### Formula : 
    conversion_rate = (completed orders / website sessions) x 100

### Calculation : 
    conversion_rate = (750 / 25000) x 100
    conversion_rate = 3%

### Business interpretation : 
There are 3 completed orders for every 100 website sessions, using the standard session-based calculation. This is a session conversion rate, not necessarily the percentage of unique visitors who purchased.

## 2. Cart abandonment rate :

`First, calculate abandoned carts :`

    abandonment_carts = 1000 - 300 
    abandonment_carts = 700

### Formula : 
    cart_abandonment_rate = (carts_created - carts_converted / carts_created) x 100

### Calculation : 
    cart_abandonment_rate = 700/1000 x 100
    cart_abandonment_rate = 70%

### Business interpretation : 
70% of created shopping carts did not convert into purchases according to the supplied figures. This indicates an opportunity to investigate checkout friction and purchasing barriers.



`Is a 3% conversion rate necessarily good or bad?`

**No**. A 3% conversion rate cannot be judged in isolation. It depends on:
- Industry and product category.
- Traffic source and visitor intent.
- Device type, such as mobile versus desktop.
- Product pricing and customer location.
- Historical performance and the store's conversion target.

The business should compare its rate with its own previous periods and relevant industry benchmarks.

`Two possible reasons for high cart abandonment :`

- **Unexpected costs :** 
Customers discover shipping fees, taxes, or additional charges during checkout.
- **Checkout friction :**
A complicated checkout, forced account creation, payment failures, or a lack of preferred payment options discourages customers from completing their orders.

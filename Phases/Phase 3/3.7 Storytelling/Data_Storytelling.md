# Phase 3.7 — Data Storytelling
## Turn Charts into Decisions and Recommendations


## 1. What is data storytelling?

Data storytelling combines three elements:

1. **Data — What happened?**  
   Example: Revenue decreased by 12% this month.
2. **Insight — What does it mean, and why does it matter?**  
   Example: The decline is concentrated in one product category. This identifies where to investigate, but does not prove the cause.
3. **Action — What should the business do next?**  
   Example: Investigate that category's sales, pricing, stock availability, and marketing performance before reallocating the budget.

A chart describes numbers. A good data story helps people make better decisions.

## 2. The Insight → Evidence → Decision framework

Use this framework when interpreting a chart or KPI.

| Step | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Observation | What do I see? | Profit margin is 35%. |
| Comparison | Compared with what? | The target was 40%. |
| Insight | What does it mean? | Margin is 5 percentage points below target. |
| Recommendation | What should happen? | Review discounts and product costs. |
| Measurement | How will we evaluate success? | Track margin against the 40% target next month. |

**Important:** A chart can show where a problem exists without proving why it happened. Treat possible causes as hypotheses until they are verified.

## 3. Five example insights and recommendations

These figures are illustrative examples, not findings from a real dataset.

### Insight 1 — Revenue is declining
- **Evidence:** Monthly revenue decreased from Rs. 500,000 to Rs. 440,000, a 12% drop.
- **Recommended action:** Compare order volume, average order value, and traffic with the previous month to locate the decline before changing marketing spend.
- **Measure success:** Track revenue, order volume, and average order value over the next review period.

### Insight 2 — A category has low profitability
- **Evidence:** Category B generates 30% of revenue but only 10% of total profit.
- **Recommended action:** Review category-level costs, discounts, and product margins. Adjust pricing or sourcing only where the analysis supports it.
- **Measure success:** Track category profit and margin after any changes.

### Insight 3 — Sales are concentrated in a few products
- **Evidence:** Five products account for 65% of total sales.
- **Recommended action:** Monitor stock levels for these products, prepare replenishment plans, and identify alternatives to reduce dependency.
- **Measure success:** Track stockouts, sales, and the share of revenue from the top products.

### Insight 4 — Repeat purchases are low
- **Evidence:** Only 20% of eligible customers made another purchase within the defined period.
- **Recommended action:** Test a targeted retention campaign for eligible first-time buyers and compare repeat-purchase rates against a control group.
- **Measure success:** Compare repeat-purchase rates for the campaign and control groups.

### Insight 5 — Regional performance differs
- **Evidence:** Region A has higher sales but a lower profit margin than Region B.
- **Recommended action:** Compare delivery costs, discounts, returns, and product mix by region before deciding where to expand.
- **Measure success:** Track regional profit, margin, and sales after any changes.

## 4. How to write a professional recommendation

A useful recommendation should be specific, measurable, and linked to evidence.

**Weak:** “Increase sales.”

**Better:** “Analyze the products responsible for the 12% monthly revenue decline, identify the largest contributing factors, and test one targeted campaign. Evaluate the change using revenue, conversion rate, and profit margin over the next four weeks.”

Include these five elements in a recommendation:

- **Action:** What should the business do?
- **Reason:** Which insight supports it?
- **Owner:** Who should do it?
- **Metric:** How will success be measured?
- **Timeframe:** When will results be reviewed?

## 5. Practice assignment — E-commerce business story

Use the following example data to write five insights and five recommended actions.

| Business metric | Previous month | Current month |
|---|---:|---:|
| Revenue | Rs. 500,000 | Rs. 450,000 |
| Orders | 1,000 | 900 |
| Profit | Rs. 150,000 | Rs. 117,000 |
| Profit margin | 30% | 26% |
| Returning customer rate | 25% | 20% |

For each insight and recommendation:
1. State what changed and support it with numerical evidence.
2. Explain why it matters to the business.
3. Recommend a relevant action.
4. Define a success metric and timeframe.
5. Avoid treating a possible cause as a proven cause.

### Calculation check

| Metric | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---:|
| Returning customer rate | 20% − 25% | −5 percentage points |
| Revenue | (450,000 − 500,000) / 500,000 | −10% |
| Orders | (900 − 1,000) / 1,000 | −10% |
| Profit | (117,000 − 150,000) / 150,000 | −22% |
| Profit margin | 26% − 30% | −4 percentage points |
| Average order value, previous month | 500,000 / 1,000 | Rs. 500 |
| Average order value, current month | 450,000 / 900 | Rs. 500 |

**Interpretation:** Revenue declined at the same rate as order volume, while average order value stayed at Rs. 500. This is useful evidence for deciding what to investigate next.

## 6. Common mistakes to avoid

- Reporting a number without explaining its business meaning.
- Confusing percentage changes with percentage-point changes.
- Assuming that fewer orders necessarily means fewer customers.
- Claiming that discounts or costs caused a profit decline without checking the data.
- Recommending promotions without considering their effect on profit margin.
- Giving vague recommendations such as “improve sales.”
- Failing to specify a target, metric, and timeframe.
- Focusing on revenue alone when profit and customer retention also matter.

## 7. Completion checklist

- [ ] Identify five meaningful insights from charts and KPIs.
- [ ] Support each insight with numerical evidence.
- [ ] Write five relevant, actionable recommendations.
- [ ] Define a success metric and timeframe for each action.
- [ ] Distinguish confirmed facts from hypotheses.
- [ ] Explain how the analysis supports a business decision.

### Key takeaway

**Insight = evidence + interpretation. Recommendation = a justified action + a way to measure whether it worked.**

Use this framework whenever you turn Power BI visuals or analytical results into a business story.
